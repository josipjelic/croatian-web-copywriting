# Duh hrvatskog jezika — the spirit of Croatian web copy

This is the soul of the skill. Web copy fails in Croatian not on grammar but on
*idiom* — it sounds translated. This file is how you make it sound *written*.

## 1. Oslovljavanje

The choice English never makes you make: **how you address the reader.** Croatian has
a formal/plural "Vi" and an informal/singular "ti", and the choice colours every
imperative, pronoun, and verb on the site. Decide once, apply everywhere.

### Vi — the professional default

Capitalized in direct address (`Hvala Vam`, `Vaš upit`). Use for businesses, B2B,
public sector, trades, finance, healthcare, anything whose value is *trust and
competence*. This is the safe default for almost every company site.

- CTA: `Zatražite ponudu` · `Pošaljite upit` · `Saznajte više` · `Kontaktirajte nas`
- Address: `Javite nam se` · `Vaš projekt vodimo od ideje do ključa`

### ti — the friendly register

Singular imperative. Use for youthful B2C brands, apps, startups, lifestyle, anything
that wants to feel like a peer, not an institution.

- CTA: `Zatraži ponudu` · `Pošalji upit` · `Saznaj više` · `Kreni`
- Address: `Javi nam se` · `Tvoj projekt vodimo od ideje do ključa`

### Impersonal — dodging the choice

Croatian can simply not address the reader — using infinitives, verbal nouns, or
first-person-plural company voice. Useful for institutional tone or when "Vi" feels
too sales-y. Note that the *polite plural imperative and the impersonal often look
identical* (`Zatražite ponudu` works as both), which is why Vi is such a safe default.

- `Naručivanje betona` (heading, no address) · `Gradimo od temelja do krova` (we-voice)

### The cardinal rule: never mix

The loudest amateur tell in Croatian copy is register drift — `Zatražite ponudu` in
the nav, `Pošalji upit` on the form button, `Saznaj više` on a card. When you touch a
site, **sweep every imperative and pronoun** and force them into one register. This is
the first thing to check and the most common thing to fix.

> **Real-world example:** the L Projekt site uses formal Vi consistently —
> `Zatražite ponudu`, `Pošaljite upit`, `Saznajte više`, `Pogledajte projekte` — but
> its contact-form button reads `Pošalji upit` (informal). That single word breaks the
> register. The fix: `Pošaljite upit`.

## 2. Prevodilački hrvatski

Translationese: sentences that exist only because an English original existed. These
are the patterns to recognize and kill. The cure is never a better translation — it's
to name the *intent* and say it natively.

### Calqued slogans (the worst offenders)

| English-brained ❌ | Native Croatian ✅ |
|---|---|
| Podignite svoje poslovanje na višu razinu | Recite konkretnu korist: `Naplatite brže, bez papira` |
| Otključajte svoj potencijal | `Iskoristite ono što već imate` (ili izbaci — fraza je prazna) |
| Vaše zadovoljstvo nam je na prvom mjestu | `Ne plaćate dok niste zadovoljni` (dokaz umjesto fraze) |
| Mi smo posvećeni pružanju kvalitete | `Kvaliteta nam je na prvom mjestu` ili samo `Gradimo kvalitetno` |
| Dobrodošli na našu web stranicu | (izbaci — nitko to ne čita; počni od koristi) |
| Vaš pouzdani partner za sve vaše potrebe | `Pouzdan partner u graditeljstvu od 2017.` (konkretno, bez "sve vaše") |
| Pružamo rješenja po mjeri | Reci koje: `Beton po mjeri — standardni, armirani, pumpani` |

### Pattern translations that read as robotic

| English pattern | Lazy calque ❌ | Native ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| Get started | Započnite odmah / Dobijte početak | `Krenite` · `Zatražite ponudu` · `Otvorite račun` |
| Learn more | Naučite više | `Saznajte više` (this one is correct — "saznati", not "naučiti") |
| Click here | Kliknite ovdje | name the action: `Pogledajte projekte`, `Preuzmite katalog` |
| Read more | Pročitajte više | `Saznajte više` · `Cijela priča` · `Nastavi čitati` (ti) |
| Contact us | Kontaktirajte nas | correct, but `Javite nam se` is warmer |
| Our solutions | Naša rješenja | usually empty — name the actual thing: `Naše usluge`, `Što gradimo` |
| Powered by | Pokretano od strane | `U pogonu:` · `Tehnologija:` · or just the logo |
| Coming soon | Dolazi uskoro | `Uskoro` · `Radimo na tome` |

### The possessive plague

English sprinkles "your/our" everywhere; Croatian doesn't. Each `vaš/svoj/naš` is
guilty until proven necessary. Croatian marks possession through context and case, so
literal possessives pile up and sound foreign.

- ❌ `Unaprijedite svoje poslovanje s našim rješenjima za vašu tvrtku`
- ✅ `Unaprijedite poslovanje` — the reader knows whose.
- ❌ `Pošaljite nam svoj upit i naš tim će vam odgovoriti`
- ✅ `Pošaljite upit i javljamo se u roku od 24 sata`

Keep the possessive only when it carries real contrast or warmth (`Vaš projekt` as a
deliberate, singular promise — not as wallpaper).

## 3. Anglizmi

Croatian has good native words; reaching for the English one reads as careless. Use
the Croatian equivalent unless the borrowed term is genuinely standard in the field.

| Avoid ❌ | Prefer ✅ |
|---|---|
| Dom (as "Home" nav link) | `Naslovna` · `Početna` |
| Apdejt / update | `ažuriranje`, `novost` |
| Bukirati | `rezervirati` |
| Daunlodati | `preuzeti` |
| Implementacija (often) | `uvođenje`, `izvedba`, `primjena` |
| Podrška (for "support" line) | fine, but `pomoć` is warmer for customers |
| Event | `događaj` |
| Tim / team | `tim` is accepted and natural in Croatian — keep it |
| Web / sajt | `mrežna stranica` / `web-stranica` (both ok); avoid `sajt` |
| Lokacija | fine, but `adresa` when you mean a street address |

Borrowed terms that ARE standard and should stay: `beton`, `tim`, `e-mail`/`e-pošta`,
`PDF`, `aplikacija`. The test is whether a professional in that field would use it in
speech. If yes, keep it; if it's only there because of the English source, replace it.

## 4. Administrativni stil

Croatian institutional writing loves nominalizations, passives, and legalese. It's the
opposite of good web copy. Marketing text breathes through *verbs* and *active voice*.

| Bureaucratic ❌ | Alive ✅ |
|---|---|
| vršenje usluga / vršimo uslugu prijevoza | `prevozimo` |
| realizacija projekata | `gradimo`, `izvodimo` |
| u svrhu poboljšanja | `da poboljšamo`, `kako bismo poboljšali` |
| putem naše mrežne stranice | `na našoj stranici` |
| sukladno / temeljem / u skladu s | `prema`, `po` (keep "sukladno" only in legal text) |
| po pitanju kvalitete | `kad je riječ o kvaliteti`, or just `kvaliteta` |
| izvršiti narudžbu | `naručiti` |
| u mogućnosti smo ponuditi | `nudimo` |
| poduzeti korake | `napravimo`, `riješimo` |

Rule of thumb: if a noun ends in **-anje / -enje / -acija / -ost** and there's a verb
hiding inside it, use the verb. `Pružanje usluge dostave` → `Dostavljamo`.

Passive → active: `Radovi se izvode prema normama` is acceptable for spec text, but a
hero is stronger active — `Radove izvodimo prema normama` / `Gradimo po normama`.

### Preciznost: znati vs poznavati (i njoj slične zamke)

Native precision is what most separates *written* from *translated*. The
highest-frequency slip in business copy: **znati** = to know a *fact* or *how to*
(`znamo to izraditi`, `znate da…`); **poznavati** = to be *familiar with* a person,
place, or domain. You know a fact, but you *poznajete* a client's business.

- ❌ `osoba koja zna vaše poslovanje` (zvuči kao „zna *da* vaše poslovanje postoji")
- ✅ `osoba koja poznaje Vaše poslovanje` (zna kako ono funkcionira)

The reflex generalizes: reach for the exact Croatian verb, not the one that maps 1:1
onto English. `make` / `get` / `do` / `provide` rarely translate straight — pick
`izraditi` / `dobiti` / `obaviti` / `pružiti` by meaning, not by the English word.

### Upitne rečenice: glagol naprijed, ne „da li"

Questions in standard Croatian don't open with **da li** — that opener is typical of
neighbouring languages and is discouraged here. And the fix isn't always „je li" either:
**je li** is correct only when the verb actually *is* `je` (biti). The reliable rule is to
**front the predicate (the verb) and attach the enclitic `li`** — the predicate carries
the sentence, so when you ask, put it first.

- ❌ `Da li trebate ponudu?` → ✅ `Trebate li ponudu?`
- ❌ `Da li želite nastaviti?` → ✅ `Želite li nastaviti?`
- ❌ `Da li je to uključeno?` → ✅ `Je li to uključeno?` (glagol je `je` → „je li" je ovdje ispravan)

Applies to every question on the page — a headline (`Trebate li brzu ponudu?`), a CTA,
an FAQ entry, a confirmation dialog.

## 5. Ton

Croatian business audiences respond to **competence, concreteness, and reliability** —
and they are *allergic to hype*. American-style superlatives ("amazing",
"revolutionary", "world-class") translate into Croatian as cheap and untrustworthy.

**Cut these unless literally provable:** `vrhunski`, `nevjerojatan`, `revolucionaran`,
`najbolji`, `vodeći`, `jedinstven`, `inovativan` (when decorative). One earned
superlative survives; five decorative ones sink the page.

**What earns trust in Croatian copy instead:**
- **Numbers and facts** — `200+ izgrađenih objekata`, `od 2017.`, `tim od 90
  stručnjaka`, `10 000+ m³ betona godišnje`. Specifics are the native flex.
- **Concreteness** — name what you do: `iskopi, betonaže temelja, zidanje, krovovi,
  fasade` beats `cjelovita građevinska rješenja`.
- **Locality and rootedness** — Croatian SMB buyers value the local, the personal, the
  known. `u Pleternici`, `partner u graditeljstvu Slavonije` signals real, not faceless.
- **Plain warmth** — `Javite nam se — odgovaramo brzo` does more than any adjective.

The voice to aim for: a skilled, straight-talking Croatian professional who respects
the reader's time. Warm, direct, specific. Confident without shouting.

## 6. Formatiranje

The mechanical tells. Getting these wrong marks copy as translated or unproofed even
when the words are fine.

### Sentence case for headlines — never Title Case

Croatian capitalizes only the first word and proper nouns. Title Case is the single
most common "machine-translated from English" giveaway.

- ✅ `Beton po mjeri — brzo, kvalitetno i povoljno`
- ❌ `Beton Po Mjeri — Brzo, Kvalitetno i Povoljno`

This applies to H1, H2, eyebrows, buttons, nav, card titles — everything.

### Diacritics — always

`č ć ž š đ` are mandatory. `Zatražite` not `Zatrazite`, `Pošaljite` not `Posaljite`.
Stripped diacritics read as spam or laziness and can change meaning.

### Numbers

- Thousands: a space (preferred for big numbers) or a period — `10 000` or `10.000`.
  Pick one and be consistent. Never the English comma (`10,000`).
- Decimals: a comma — `3,5 m`, `99,9 %`.
- Percent and units: a space before the unit — `50 %`, `36 m`, `11 m³`. (`%` with a
  space is the Croatian norm.)
- Ranges: en dash, no spaces around it for numbers — `5–11 m³`, `07:00–15:00`,
  `Pon–Pet`.

### Currency — euro since 2023

Croatia adopted the euro on 1 January 2023. Use it.

- `1.500 €` or `1.500 eura` — symbol/word *after* the number, with a space.
- Kuna (`kn`) appears ONLY inside verbatim historical or legal facts you must not alter
  (e.g. a registered `Temeljni kapital: 20.000,00 kn`). Never in new marketing copy.

### Dates

- `22. lipnja 2026.` — day with a period, **month lowercase in the genitive**, year
  with a trailing period. Month names are never capitalized in Croatian.
- Numeric: `22. 6. 2026.` (spaces after the periods) or `22.06.2026.`
- Never the English `June 22, 2026` order.

### Quotation marks and dashes

- Quotes: „low-high" — opening „ at the baseline, closing " up top: `„ključ u ruke"`.
  Avoid English "straight" or "curly" quotes.
- Em dash `—` for asides and punchy headline breaks (`Gradimo pouzdano — od temelja do
  krova`). Hyphen `-` only inside words (`web-stranica`).

## 7. Slaganje s brojevima

Croatian numbers govern the case of the noun, and getting this wrong is glaring —
especially in stats blocks, which web copy is full of. The pattern for the counted noun:

- **1** (and anything ending in 1 except 11): nominative singular — `1 objekt`, `21 dan`
- **2, 3, 4** (and …2/3/4 except 12–14): genitive singular / "paucal" — `2 objekta`,
  `3 dana`, `24 sata`
- **5+** (and 11–14, and 0): genitive plural — `5 objekata`, `90 djelatnika`, `10 dana`
- **"200+", "50+", "10 000+"**: read as the big-number form → genitive plural —
  `200+ objekata`, `50+ vozila`, `90 djelatnika`, `10 000+ m³ betona`

Common stat-line forms, correct:

- `200+ izgrađenih objekata` · `90 iskusnih djelatnika` · `50+ vozila i radnih strojeva`
  · `10 000+ m³ betona godišnje` · `Odgovaramo u roku od 24 sata`

When the number is decorative ("badge" style), still agree the noun with it — a stat
that reads `90 djelatnik` or `200 objekt` instantly looks machine-made.
